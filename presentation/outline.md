## Slide 1. Corruption robustness of AI-generated image detectors

### On slide

**Title** Corruption robustness of AI-generated image detectors

**Text fragments**

- Shared-image corruption
- Blur $\sigma=0.6$ recall. CNN **0.3916**, ResNet-18 **0.3143**
- Augmentation transfer without clean cost?
- Architecture or training signal?

**Visual** Small clean-versus-blur $\sigma=0.6$ image pair cropped from `report/figures/fig1_corruptions.pdf`. Use the title, literature framing, and hypotheses from `report/report.tex`.

### Script

**Katie, 0:00 to 0:50**

Messaging and social platforms routinely add JPEG recompression, resizing, blur, and noise. A detector that only works on clean images therefore protects nothing once an image is shared. We test two hypotheses. First, noise-plus-JPEG augmentation may transfer to blur that no model saw during training without costing clean accuracy. Second, ImageNet pretraining may make ResNet-18 inherently more robust than a CNN trained from scratch.

**Partner, 0:50 to 1:30**

Accuracy alone hides the deployment failure. We treat FAKE as the positive class, so low recall means generated images pass as real. At held-out blur $\sigma=0.6$, baseline recall collapses to 0.3916 for the CNN and 0.3143 for ResNet-18, despite clean accuracies of 0.9442 and 0.9693. This gives us the research question. Which matters more under corruption, architecture or the training signal?

## Slide 2. CIFAKE and the held-out corruption protocol

### On slide

**Title** CIFAKE and the held-out corruption protocol

**Text fragments**

- **120,000** balanced **32×32** RGB images
- **100,000** train • **20,000** test
- **16** conditions • **5** metrics
- Blur held out

**Visual** `report/figures/fig1_corruptions.pdf` as the main visual. Use the Dataset and Experimental Protocol sections of `report/report.tex` as the content source.

### Script

**Katie, 0:00 to 1:30**

CIFAKE contains 120,000 balanced 32×32 RGB images. It pairs 60,000 CIFAR-10 photographs with 60,000 Stable Diffusion v1.4 fakes. The fixed split gives us 100,000 training images and 20,000 test images.

We evaluate each run under 16 conditions and 5 metrics, producing 192 metric rows. The conditions are clean images plus five severities each of Gaussian noise, JPEG compression, and Gaussian blur. The robust strategy trains with noise, JPEG, and horizontal flips at $p=0.5$, but it never sees blur. We reject training on blur because holding it out turns the blur evaluation into a transfer test rather than a memorisation test.

The visual shows why severity matters at this resolution. Blur $\sigma=0.6$ already removes most pixel-level texture from a 32×32 image, while JPEG remains gentler at the headline quality $q=40$.

## Slide 3. Two architectures and a paired experimental design

### On slide

**Title** Two architectures and a paired experimental design

**Text fragments**

- CNN • **544,066** parameters
- Pretrained ResNet-18 • **11,169,858** parameters
- **2 × 2 × 3 = 12** runs
- Paired seeds **42 • 43 • 44**
- One controlled training recipe

**Visual** Use `presentation/fig4_architectures.pdf`, generated by `presentation/make_arch_figure.py` from the architecture definitions in `220231053_251104382_Group_Neural_Networks_Cifake.ipynb`. The figure shows both pipelines, output shapes, parameter counts, and the adapted ResNet-18 stem and head callouts.

### Script

**Katie, 0:00 to 2:00**

We compare two architectures. The BaselineCNN has 544,066 parameters and learns from scratch. The ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 has 11,169,858 parameters. Each model maps a 3×32×32 image to 2 logits.

We adapt ResNet-18 to the small CIFAKE images with a 3×3 stride-1 CIFAR stem, no max-pooling, and a 2-class head. We fine-tune the full network rather than freezing it because we want its representation to adapt to real-versus-fake evidence.

The matrix crosses 2 architectures with 2 strategies and 3 paired seeds, giving 12 runs. Seeds 42, 43, and 44 use identical splits and initial weights across each baseline and robust pair. The resulting accuracy gaps are paired differences, so split and initialisation do not confound the strategy comparison.

We hold the remaining recipe fixed. Every run uses Adam, batch size 128, at most 30 epochs, patience 5, and the checkpoint with the lowest clean validation loss. The learning rate is $10^{-3}$ for the CNN and $10^{-4}$ for ResNet-18.

## Slide 4. Augmentation, not pretraining, drives robustness

### On slide

**Title** Augmentation, not pretraining, drives robustness

**Text fragments**

- Noise $\sigma=0.10$ • CNN **+0.1309** • ResNet-18 **+0.2722**
- Held-out blur $\sigma=0.6$ • CNN **+0.1442** • ResNet-18 **+0.2171**
- Clean • CNN **+0.0049** • ResNet-18 **+0.0039**
- Pretraining ≠ robustness
- Blur recall collapse • **0.3143**

**Visual** Two-panel layout. Show `report/figures/fig2_accuracy_severity.pdf` on the left. Show a simplified redraw of Table III from `report/report.tex` on the right, sourced directly from `report/headline_metrics.csv`. Take every paired gap, SD, and minimum from `report/gap_stats.csv`.

### Script

**Partner, beat 1, 0:00 to 1:00. Seen and unseen corruption gains**

Augmentation works on both the corruptions used in training and the held-out family. At noise $\sigma=0.10$, CNN accuracy rises from 0.8029 to 0.9338, a paired gap of +0.1309. ResNet-18 rises from 0.6893 to 0.9615, a gap of +0.2722. At JPEG $q=40$, the CNN rises from 0.8826 to 0.9282, a gap of +0.0455, and ResNet-18 rises from 0.8862 to 0.9578, a gap of +0.0716.

Transfer survives on held-out blur $\sigma=0.6$. The CNN rises from 0.6926 to 0.8367, a paired gap of +0.1442. ResNet-18 rises from 0.6566 to 0.8738, a gap of +0.2171. All 8 corrupted-condition gap means and minima are positive. The smallest minimum is +0.0363 for CNN JPEG $q=40$.

**Partner, beat 2, 1:00 to 2:00. No clean-accuracy cost**

The robust strategy does not trade away clean accuracy. The CNN rises from 0.9442 to 0.9491. Its paired clean gap is +0.0049 ± 0.0013, with a minimum of +0.0036. ResNet-18 rises from 0.9693 to 0.9732. Its paired clean gap is +0.0039 ± 0.0011, with a minimum of +0.0027. These small positive differences support no clean cost, not a meaningful clean gain.

**Partner, beat 3, 2:00 to 3:00. Pretraining buys clean accuracy, not robustness**

The baseline ResNet-18 beats the baseline CNN on clean images at 0.9693 versus 0.9442. That ordering reverses under unseen noise and blur. At noise $\sigma=0.10$, ResNet-18 scores 0.6893 versus 0.8029 for the CNN. At blur $\sigma=1.0$, it scores 0.5818 versus 0.6382. JPEG $q=40$ is the exception, where ResNet-18 retains a small lead of 0.8862 versus 0.8826.

Capacity becomes useful once the training signal is right. Robust ResNet-18 reaches 0.9615 on noise $\sigma=0.10$ and 0.8738 on held-out blur $\sigma=0.6$, paired gains of +0.2722 and +0.2171.

**Partner, beat 4, 3:00 to 4:00. Recall and calibration collapse**

At blur $\sigma=0.6$, baseline ResNet-18 records 0.6566 accuracy, 0.3143 recall, 0.9967 precision, 0.4772 F1, and 0.9484 ROC-AUC. The high ROC-AUC means the ranking survives better than the clean-calibrated decision threshold. Robust training lifts the same model to 0.8738 accuracy, 0.7541 recall, 0.8561 F1, and 0.9865 ROC-AUC.

At blur $\sigma=1.0$, baseline ResNet-18 falls to 0.5818 accuracy, 0.1735 recall, 0.2894 F1, and 0.7872 ROC-AUC. At this severity, the representation loses information as well as calibration. The baseline CNN shows the same mechanism at $\sigma=1.0$, with 0.6382 accuracy, 0.3010 recall, 0.9337 precision, 0.4499 F1, and 0.8107 ROC-AUC.

## Slide 5. Limitations and next experiments

### On slide

**Title** Limitations and next experiments

**Text fragments**

- **1** held-out family • rotate it
- **3** seeds • use **5 to 10** for confidence intervals
- **1** generator, Stable Diffusion v1.4 • evaluate a second without retraining
- **32×32** • the curves do not transfer, the design does
- Re-tune the threshold on a small corrupted validation set

**Visual** Five bordered cards pair each bound with its follow-up from the limitations section of `report/report.tex`. The threshold-recalibration card spans the width because it is the cheapest next experiment and needs no model retraining.

### Script

**Partner, 0:00 to 0:35**

We bound the result to one held-out family, three seeds, one fake generator, and 32×32 images. We would first rotate the held-out family. We would then use 5 to 10 seeds for confidence intervals and evaluate the trained detectors on fakes from a second generator without retraining. The severity-response curves do not transfer to full-resolution detectors, but the held-out design does. Alongside the rotation, we would re-tune the threshold on a small corrupted validation set. This needs no retraining and separates calibration repair from representation damage.

### On slide

**Title** What the evidence supports

**Text fragments**

- **H2 not supported** • clean **0.9693 vs 0.9442**

**Visual** Two hypothesis-verdict cards mirror slide 1's research questions and use the conclusion in `report/report.tex`. Keep the evidence-scoped verdict as the final dark banner.

### Script

**Both, 0:00 to 0:25**

The first hypothesis is supported. Noise-plus-JPEG augmentation transfers to unseen blur, with paired gains of +0.1442 for the CNN and +0.2171 for ResNet-18 at $\sigma=0.6$. Clean paired gains are +0.0049 and +0.0039, so the augmentation was close to free. The architecture hypothesis is not supported. Baseline ResNet-18 reaches 0.9693 clean accuracy against 0.9442 for the CNN, but it is more fragile than the 20 times smaller CNN under unseen noise and blur. On CIFAKE, corruption augmentation was close to free and transferred to unseen blur. Pretraining bought clean accuracy, not robustness.
